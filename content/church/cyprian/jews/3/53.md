157948Ante-Nicene Fathers/Volume V Vol. V, Cyprian, The Treatises of Cyprian,

¶ 53. That the secrets of God cannot be seen through, and therefore that our
faith ought to be simple.[^1]

In the first Epistle of Paul to the Corinthians: "We see now through the glass
in an enigma, but then with face to face. Now I know partly; but then I shall
know even as also I am known."[^2] Also in Solomon, in Wisdom: "And in
simplicity of heart seek Him."[^3] Also in the same: "He who walketh with
simplicity, walketh trustfully."[^4] Also in the same:  "Seek not things higher
than thyself, and look not into things stronger than thyself."[^5] Also in
Solomon: "Be not excessively righteous, and do not reason more than is
required."[^6]  Also in Isaiah: "Woe unto them who are convicted in themselves.
"[^7] Also in the Maccabees:  "Daniel in his simplicity was delivered from the
mouth of the lions."[^8] Also in the Epistle of Paul to the Romans: "Oh the
depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! How incomprehensible
are His judgments, and how unsearchable are His ways! For who has known the
mind of the Lord? or who has been His counsellor? or who has first given to
Him, and it shall be recompensed to him again?  Because from Him, and through
Him, and in Him, are all things: to Him be glory for ever and ever."[^9] Also
to Timothy: "But foolish and unlearned questions avoid, knowing that they
generate strifes. But the servant of God ought not to strive, but to be gentle
towards all men."[^10]
