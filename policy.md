



# Footnotes of Edited or Translated Volumes

Many public domain works, especially translations come with footnotes of the editor.
If they actually add to the text, without distracting from it, they may be maintained.

However, in many cases, editors add inane or somewhat derogatory footnotes, and I will remove them as I see fit.
For example, the editor of the translation here of Geoffrey of Monmouth's *History of the Kings of Britain* found it fitting to mock the very work he was translating whenever the history seemed fanciful or unique. Many of these footnotes have been removed, however, retained are those that clarify historical, linguistic or geographical information.
Or, for example, certain of the Protestant translators and editors of the "Schaff" Nicene Fathers collection repeatedly make unsubstantiated claims that a passage that refutes their perspective "must" be an addition or interpolation.
Of course when a text might have multiple variants,---some more loyal to the original than others,---that can be noted, but notes cannot simply flatly and falsely negate texts based on nothing but the reviewer's opinion.

The goal of this site is to present works as they truly were. Footnotes that add geographic, cultural or context notes that would help readers should be maintained.
Notes that are devoid of content other than mockery or bold and unsubstantiated denial should be removed.
